Main characters

Hiccup Horrendous Haddock III

Viking Inventor

Hiccup is a clever but physically awkward Viking teen and the son of Stoick, the chief of Berk.[4][1]

Instead of brute strength, he relies on creativity and empathy, ultimately redefining what it means to be a hero in his village.[3][1]

Toothless

Night Fury Dragon

Toothless is a rare and powerful Night Fury, feared by Vikings for his speed and plasma blasts.[4][1]

After losing part of his tail, he depends on Hiccup’s custom tail fin to fly, symbolizing their mutual trust and partnership.[1][3]

Astrid Hofferson

Viking Warrior

Astrid is one of the top dragon trainees in Berk, known for her discipline, determination, and combat skill.[4][1]

Initially suspicious of Hiccup’s secrets, she becomes one of his strongest supporters after witnessing Toothless’s true nature.[3][1]

Stoick the Vast

Chief Father

Stoick leads Berk and believes dragons are deadly enemies after years of raids on the village.[1][4]

His journey from fierce dragon hunter to accepting peace mirrors the village’s transformation and Hiccup’s influence.[3][1]

Dragon species

Night Fury

Night Furies are fast, intelligent dragons capable of firing explosive plasma blasts and vanishing into the night sky.[4][1]

They are considered the rarest and most mysterious dragons in the known world of Berk.[2][1]

Monstrous Nightmare

Monstrous Nightmares are large, intimidating dragons known for setting their entire bodies on fire during battle.[5][4]

Powerful and temperamental, they demand respect and confident handling from any Viking who approaches them.[5]

Gronckle

Gronckles are stocky, boulder-eating dragons that fly with stubby wings and can spit molten lava-like blasts.[2][4]

They enjoy affection but dislike certain kinds of scratching, showing that each dragon has unique quirks and preferences.[5]

Other dragons

The broader franchise introduces many more dragons, each with specialized abilities, appearances, and behaviors.[6][2]

These species expand the world beyond Berk, highlighting different habitats, strengths, and challenges for dragon riders.[2][5]

Dragon training guide

In the world of Berk, training a dragon is less about force and more about patience, body language, and trust-building.[5][1]

Core principles

Start by staying calm and non-threatening, letting the dragon observe you from a safe distance.[1][5]
Use small, consistent gestures and rewards, such as food or scratching favorite spots, to reinforce positive behavior.[5]
Avoid shouting or sudden movements, which can trigger a dragon’s defensive instincts and break fragile trust.[5]

Building trust

  • Approach slowly and stop if the dragon shows fear or aggression.
    • [1][5]
  • Show empty hands or even render yourself defenseless to prove peaceful intent, especially with Night Furies.
    • [1][5]
  • Offer food like fish or other treats the dragon enjoys, rewarding calm and curious behavior.
    • [5]
  • Gently touch the nose or snout only after the dragon leans in to close the distance.
    • [1][5]

Training techniques

  • Use consistent signals or calls when beginning and ending training sessions.
    • [5]
  • Practice simple tasks first, such as sitting, turning, or dropping items on command.
    • [5]
  • Scratch favorite spots, like under the chin or along the neck, as a powerful positive reinforcement.
    • [5]
  • Adopt a “work hard, play hard” routine so the dragon associates training with fun and bonding.
    • [5]

“Expert trainers recognize that the best way to train their dragons is to balance disciplined practice with playful moments of flight, games, and shared adventures.”[5]

Key themes

Friendship and understanding

Hiccup and Toothless show that enemies can become allies when they are given a chance to understand each other beyond fear and prejudice.[3][1]

Their bond slowly convinces Berk that dragons are not just threats but partners who can help protect the village.[2][1]

Challenging tradition

For generations, Vikings believed their only choice was to fight dragons, and questioning that belief made Hiccup an outcast.[3][1]

By choosing empathy over violence, Hiccup proves that changing old traditions can create a safer, stronger community for everyone.[2][1]

The story has grown into a full media franchise with sequels, series, and a planned live‑action remake, expanding the world of Berk and its dragon riders.[7][2]
